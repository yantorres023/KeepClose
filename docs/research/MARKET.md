# MARKET

## Problem space (FACT unless marked)
- 47% of US adults lost touch with at least a few friends in the past year; 12% report no close friends (up from 3% in 1990) [S4]. Pew (2023, via [S5] coverage): 8% no close friends.
- Friendship closeness decays without contact; kin ties are more resilient [S3].
- People underestimate how much others appreciate being reached out to [S2].
- Even motivated people mostly don't reach out to old friends; awkwardness and fear of being unwanted are barriers [S1].
- Surgeon General (2023) frames social disconnection as a public-health issue [S5].

## Market size
Published "personal CRM market" reports range from ~$0.5B to $16.5B [S23] — mutually
inconsistent and methodologically opaque. **We do not rely on them.** INFERENCE: the
friend/family segment is a small consumer-utility niche; the money in "personal CRM" is in
professional networking (Dex, Mesh).

## Bottom-up sanity check (HYPOTHESIS, illustrative only)
If a solo-developer app reached 50,000 lifetime installs, 25% activate, and 3% of those buy
a $9.99 one-time unlock → ~375 purchases ≈ $3.7K gross before store fees. Viable as an indie
product only with very low costs (local-first, no backend) — which is our architecture.

## Segments (HYPOTHESIS)
1. **Busy "caring but forgetful" adults (25–45)** — life transitions (new job, move, new parent) compress attention. Primary.
2. **Long-distance friends/family / expats** — contact requires intent; no shared routines.
3. **People who want to be a more attentive friend** — self-improvement motivated; overlaps productivity audiences.
4. Excluded: networkers/sales (different job, better-served), people seeking companionship (we do not replace relationships).

## Trends
- Platform owners adding AI follow-up suggestions (iOS 26) [S26] — raises expectations, threatens generic reminders, but requires reading messages.
- Privacy-first, no-account indie apps are proliferating [S17, S19, S20].
- Personal CRM consolidation: Clay → Automattic [S13]; UpHabit → sales [S22].

## Market conclusion
Real, well-documented human problem; weak evidence of willingness to pay; crowded with
cadence apps. Opportunity exists only for a sharper wedge with a natural trigger and
near-zero setup. See RED_TEAM.md.
